Strategic Industrial Participation & Offsets

A practitioner's masterclass for people new to Offset and Industrial Participation. It sets academic theory aside and concentrates on the commercial realities: winning bids, protecting margins and delivering what was promised.

Who the course is for

People taking on Offset or Industrial Participation responsibilities for the first time: bid and capture managers, newly appointed Offset managers, commercial, contracts and procurement staff, programme managers, finance staff who cost and report Offset, and executives who approve bids.

It suits defence and aerospace companies bidding into markets with Offset requirements, their suppliers, and UK companies and government staff who work with foreign suppliers. No prior knowledge of Offset is needed.

Course aim. To turn Offset obligations from a compliance cost into a competitive advantage that wins bids and protects margins.

How much do you already know?

Six questions that experienced Offset managers should get right. It takes about two minutes, and nothing you choose is recorded.

6 questions

  1. A sales agent you hired bribes an official to speed up approval of your Offset project. Nobody at your company knew. Under the UK Bribery Act, your company:

    Answer: C. Section 7 makes a company liable when someone performing services for it pays a bribe to win or keep business for it, anywhere in the world. Its only defence is adequate procedures to prevent bribery.

  2. A local supplier invoices you £10m for new work. 40% of its value is imported material, and £2m of the order is business you already place with that supplier every year. Under a typical value-added rule, how much is eligible for credit before any multiplier?

    Answer: D. Many regimes credit only new business, and only the share of value added locally: (£10m − £2m) × 60% = £4.8m.

  3. Under Poland’s Offset Act, what penalty applies to an obligation left unperformed?

    Answer: A. Poland’s penalty equals the unperformed obligation, and the supplier’s security must be at least the Offset value. Many other regimes charge between 5% and 15%.

  4. Since 2026, which activity earns the highest multiplier under Canada’s Industrial and Technological Benefits policy?

    Answer: B. Canada raised Indigenous workforce development to ten times its value in 2026, its largest multiplier. Investment in Canadian-owned intellectual property earns five times, and direct work with small firms twice.

  5. Switzerland set the Offset on its F-35A purchase at 60% of the contract value. Which rule also decides where the work goes?

    Answer: D. Swiss Offsets follow a regional key: 65% to German-speaking, 30% to French-speaking and 5% to Italian-speaking Switzerland.

  6. Your Offset project would share US-origin technical data for a defence item with a foreign partner. Which approval do you normally need before sharing it?

    Answer: A. Under the US International Traffic in Arms Regulations, defence services and technical data need State Department approval, usually through a technical assistance agreement, before they are shared.

What participants will be able to do

  1. Explain the deal

    What Offset, Industrial Participation and localisation are, why governments use them and how they differ by country.

  2. Read a policy

    Pick out the clauses in a national Offset policy that drive cost, risk and strategy.

  3. Build a strategy

    Shape an Offset strategy for a bid, and judge how it changes the chance of winning.

  4. Place work locally

    Design direct Offset and localisation projects that protect quality and schedule.

  5. Transfer technology safely

    Design technology, research, investment and training projects that earn credit, protect core intellectual property and stay within export controls.

  6. Do the numbers

    Calculate credit, multipliers, banked credit, penalty exposure and the true cost of an Offset programme.

  7. Negotiate

    Prepare for and conduct a negotiation with an Offset authority.

  8. Deliver cleanly

    Run a programme to close-out: governance, evidence, audits, reporting and recovery, within anti-bribery law.

The capstone: Project Halcyon

A live negotiation that draws on every module.

Every exercise in the course builds towards one fictional procurement: a £500m helicopter programme with a demanding Offset authority. In the capstone, teams play the foreign supplier's capture and Offset team and negotiate the Industrial Participation agreement against the clock.

Developments during the negotiation test judgement as well as technique. Afterwards, each participant turns the result into a strategy paper for the board.

  • TeamsFour or five people, each in a defined role
  • Time2.5 hours online, or up to a full day in-house
  • Across the tableThe Offset authority, played by the course leader
  • Scored onThe agreement reached, what the plan costs and covers, and how the team conducts itself

Assessment

  • A knowledge check after each module: eight questions, with a pass mark of six.
