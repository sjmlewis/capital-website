Strategic Industrial Participation & Offsets

A practitioner's masterclass for people new to offset and industrial participation. It sets academic theory aside and concentrates on the commercial realities: winning bids, protecting margins and delivering what was promised.

Register your interestSee the six modules

Six weeks online, or three to four days in-house for your team

Who the course is for

Corporate executives, bid managers and procurement professionals, and in particular people taking on offset or industrial participation responsibilities for the first time.

It suits defence and aerospace companies bidding into markets with offset or industrial participation requirements, and suppliers that want to understand the commitments their customers have made.

Course aim. To turn offset obligations from a compliance cost into a competitive advantage that wins bids and protects margins.

Six modules

Each module is built around a case study from real programmes, worked through in a live session.

  1. Module 1

    The commercial reality of global offsets

    How offset markets really operate, beyond the legal texts.

    • The myth of the open market. How governments use structural barriers, not only the WTO Government Procurement Agreement, to secure local economic benefit.
    • Regulated and transactional markets. Working in tightly regulated markets such as the EU, and in transactional markets outside the agreement, such as Saudi Arabia and Malaysia.
    • The capability override. Recognising when a government's urgent need for your capability outweighs its own preference for local industry.
  2. Module 2

    Localisation as a competitive advantage

    Direct offsets: turning local content requirements, civil and defence, into a reason to choose you.

    • From burden to asset. How local assembly or a regional headquarters can outweigh a lower price from competitors that have not localised.
    • Supply chain reality checks. The practicalities of vetting local suppliers and raising them to aerospace and defence standards.
    • Structuring the deal. Direct offset packages that meet local political goals while you keep control of quality.
  3. Module 3

    Technology transfer and indirect offsets

    Meeting socio-economic obligations without giving away your core technology.

    • Defining technology transfer. What governments ask for, compared with what is commercially viable to provide.
    • Protecting intellectual property. Transfer agreements, training programmes and university partnerships that earn credit while safeguarding core IP.
    • Foreign direct investment. Joint ventures and equity investment as ways to satisfy industrial participation authorities.
  4. Module 4

    The mathematics of offsets

    Valuation and multipliers: the quantitative skills that protect the bottom line.

    • Credit valuation. How national authorities calculate the credit value of a project, compared with the cash actually spent.
    • Multipliers. Securing higher multipliers, for example £4 of credit for every £1 spent, by aligning projects with a country's most urgent economic goals.
    • Offset banking. Managing surplus credits and applying them to future campaigns to lower the cost of later bids.
  5. Module 5

    Cultural execution and negotiation

    The human side of closing an offset agreement in different parts of the world.

    • The practitioner's edge. Why standard corporate negotiating tactics fail with sovereign industrial participation authorities.
    • Regional approaches. How pitching an offset package differs in the Middle East, North America and Southeast Asia.
    • Redirecting the request. Steering a government agency from an unworkable demand towards a viable alternative, without losing the bid.
  6. Module 6

    Crisis management, audits and penalty avoidance

    Managing risk, and what to do when delivery falls short.

    • The audit trail. Internal reporting that proves causality and additionality to sceptical government auditors.
    • Managing underperformance. Renegotiating milestones and substituting projects when a local partner fails to deliver.
    • Avoiding penalties. Legitimate commercial and contractual routes to avoid liquidated damages or exclusion from future procurement.

Two ways to take the course

Six weeks

Online cohort

One module a week. Each week brings a recorded lecture of 60 to 90 minutes, required reading drawn from actual government offset guidelines, and a live 60-minute online session working through that week's case study.

Three or four days

In-house intensive

Delivered to your team, two modules a day over three days. An optional fourth day is a live negotiation exercise: your team negotiates a mock £500m offset package with a demanding procurement agency, played by the course leader.

What participants will be able to do

  • Read an offset or industrial participation requirement and judge what it will really take to meet it.
  • Choose between direct and indirect offsets, and structure a package that earns credit and protects margin.
  • Transfer technology and know-how without giving away core intellectual property.
  • Value credits, make good use of multipliers and bank surplus credit for future bids.
  • Negotiate with industrial participation authorities in different regions.
  • Keep the records auditors expect, and recover a programme that is falling behind.
