The challenge

The UK expects overseas suppliers to invest in its defence industry. Meeting that expectation means finding UK companies that can genuinely make your parts, develop your technology or support your programme, and finding them early.

A web search turns up distributors, sales offices and advertisers alongside real manufacturers. Reviewing their capabilities, financial health, compliance and certifications takes a significant investment of time.

Advisory services

How the platform works

Six steps from a parts list to first contact. Your engineers stay in control at every one of them.

  1. Upload your parts list

    Plain, unclassified descriptions. No drawings, part numbers or controlled data are needed.

  2. Choose your priorities

    Put ready-now manufacturers first, or give more weight to the UK value a partnership creates.

  3. Exclude competitors

    Remove competitor groups from your results. Anything excluded stays listed, so you can bring it back.

  4. Review the shortlist

    Results for each line are grouped by readiness, each with a reason and links to the company's own published information.

  5. Check the companies you pick

    Financial health, ownership and security information for the firms you shortlist.

  6. Make first contact in your own name

    A sanitised first-stage capability enquiry for each company, with a tracker for replies.

Built for engineers, not marketing

Every match comes with a reason that is simple to understand, and a link to the company's own published information. Companies that only sell or distribute are kept out of the manufacturer results.

Latest Offset alerts

A weekly round-up of new defence sales around the world above the Offset thresholds, and changes to the Offset rules.

Checked weekly. Last updated 26 October 2026.

See all Offset alerts
  • Poland22 October 2026

    Kongsberg wins about NOK 700m to supply weapon stations for Poland's K2 tanks

    Kongsberg has signed a contract worth about NOK 700 million with Hyundai Rotem to supply Protector RS4 remote weapon stations for the K2 tanks, recovery vehicles and engineering vehicles ordered by Poland, with a support package. The first systems are due to ship in spring 2027.

  • Morocco21 October 2026

    Morocco in advanced talks to buy the David's Sling air defence system

    Morocco's defence ministry is reported to be at a very advanced stage of negotiations with Rafael and Raytheon for the David's Sling system, including Stunner interceptors, radars, launchers, training and maintenance. No contract value has been published; Rabat is reported to be seeking industrial returns.

  • Netherlands21 October 2026

    US approves a possible US$294m sale of aircraft self-protection systems to the Netherlands

    The US State Department has approved a possible sale to the Netherlands of 16 CIRCM infrared countermeasure systems and 38 radar warning receivers, with support, estimated at US$294 million. Northrop Grumman is the principal contractor.

  • Norway20 October 2026

    Norway sets out its plan to buy up to 20 standardised vessels with the UK and Lithuania

    Norway's Defence Materiel Agency has told industry how it will buy up to 20 standardised vessels: up to 14 naval vessels for Norway, the UK and Lithuania, and up to 6 Coast Guard vessels, under two parallel contracts that one bidder cannot both win. Prequalification comes next, with award of the large-vessel contract targeted for 2027 and first delivery in 2030.

  • Denmark20 October 2026

    Denmark orders more Naval Strike Missiles from Kongsberg for NOK 400m

    Denmark has ordered a further batch of Kongsberg's Naval Strike Missiles, worth NOK 400 million (about US$42 million), in a government-to-government deal through Norway's Defence Materiel Agency. The number of missiles has not been disclosed.

  • Romania20 October 2026

    Romania signs a €920m contract for four naval ships funded by EU SAFE loans

    Romania's government has signed a €920 million contract for two 96-metre offshore patrol vessels and two diving support vessels, to be built at the Mangalia shipyard bought by a Rheinmetall and MSC consortium, with the programme due to finish by 2030. Because it is funded through the EU's SAFE loans, the purchase is exempt from Romania's Offset rules.

  • Greece Rules20 October 2026

    Greek bill would set a minimum 25% national participation in armament programmes

    A Greek defence bill due to go to cabinet and then parliament would set a minimum 25% share for Greek industry in armament programmes. The text has not yet been published, so what will count towards the share is not yet known.

  • Australia13 October 2026

    Australia seeks industry offers for a medium-range ground-based air defence system

    Australia's Department of Defence has issued an industry solicitation for Land 6504, a medium-range ground-based air defence system for the Army, with vendor selection due by the end of 2027. Australia has no Offset policy; its Australian Industry Capability rules apply instead.

  • Saudi Arabia Rules4 September 2026

    Saudi Arabia's new Government Procurement Law takes effect on 2 January 2027

    Saudi Arabia's new Government Procurement Law, published on 4 September 2026, takes effect on 2 January 2027. It gives the Local Content and Government Procurement Authority a role in planning and bid evaluation, with local content preference rules still to be issued.
