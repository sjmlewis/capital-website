The challenge

The UK expects overseas suppliers to invest in its defence industry. Meeting that expectation means finding UK companies that can genuinely make your parts, develop your technology or support your programme, and finding them early.

A web search turns up distributors, sales offices and advertisers alongside real manufacturers. Reviewing their capabilities, financial health, compliance and certifications takes a significant investment of time.

Advisory services

How the platform works

Six steps from a parts list to first contact. Your engineers stay in control at every one of them.

  1. Upload your parts list

    Plain, unclassified descriptions. No drawings, part numbers or controlled data are needed.

  2. Choose your priorities

    Put ready-now manufacturers first, or give more weight to the UK value a partnership creates.

  3. Exclude competitors

    Remove competitor groups from your results. Anything excluded stays listed, so you can bring it back.

  4. Review the shortlist

    Results for each line are grouped by readiness, each with a reason and links to the company's own published information.

  5. Check the companies you pick

    Financial health, ownership and security information for the firms you shortlist.

  6. Make first contact in your own name

    A sanitised first-stage capability enquiry for each company, with a tracker for replies.

Built for engineers, not marketing

Every match comes with a reason that is simple to understand, and a link to the company's own published information. Companies that only sell or distribute are kept out of the manufacturer results.

  • Malaysia Rules14 October 2026

    Malaysia's Offset cap on CPTPP-covered purchases falls from 60% to 40% on 28 November (opens the source in a new tab)

    Under Malaysia's schedule to the CPTPP trade pact, which took effect for Malaysia on 29 November 2022, Offset credits on purchases by the covered ministries are capped at 60% of contract value for the first four years, then 40% until the end of the eighth year, 20% until the twelfth, and none after that. The first step down therefore falls on 28 November 2026. Defence purchases are largely outside the pact, so the full Industrial Collaboration Programme still applies to them.

    Source: CPTPP, Malaysia schedule↗ (opens in a new tab)

  • United Kingdom Rules13 October 2026

    Still no government response on the UK's planned defence Offsets regime (opens the source in a new tab)

    The Ministry of Defence's written consultation on a defence Offsets regime closed on 23 December 2025. As of 13 October 2026 the consultation page still says the feedback is being analysed, and no outcome has been published. Overseas suppliers bidding into UK programmes should plan for UK workshare and Industrial Participation commitments while the rules are settled.

    Source: GOV.UK↗ (opens in a new tab)

  • Malaysia Civil9 October 2026

    Malaysia's Budget 2027 funds a RM3.4bn rail overhaul and 42 new trains (opens the source in a new tab)

    Budget 2027 gives Prasarana more than RM3.4 billion to rehabilitate its urban rail network and funds 42 new train sets for the ETS and KTM Komuter services. Malaysia's Industrial Collaboration Programme applies to purchases paid with federal funds worth RM50 million or more from foreign contractors, which usually includes rolling stock and signalling.

    Source: Bernama↗ (opens in a new tab)

  • Denmark9 October 2026

    Denmark takes delivery of the first of four new air defence systems (opens the source in a new tab)

    Denmark's defence procurement agency has received the first of four ground-based air defence systems bought under a DKK 58.2 billion programme, with support contracts still to be signed. Denmark assesses industrial co-operation case by case on purchases from foreign suppliers worth more than DKK 50 million.

    Source: Nordic Defence Sector↗ (opens in a new tab)

  • Romania7 October 2026

    Romania orders 12 Airbus H225M helicopters under the EU's SAFE defence loans (opens the source in a new tab)

    Airbus has announced an order from Romania for 12 H225M helicopters, placed through the French armaments agency and funded by the EU's SAFE loans, with the Romanian maker IAR as its long-term partner. Romania's technological and industrial cooperation rules cover government-to-government purchases, and the Brașov workforce has already questioned how much work will come to Romania.

    Source: Airbus↗ (opens in a new tab)

  • Peru5 October 2026

    Peru moves to fund a second batch of 12 F-16 Block 70 fighters (opens the source in a new tab)

    Peru's government has asked Congress for extra credit to pay for a second batch of 12 F-16s from Lockheed Martin, taking the fleet to 24. The money must be spent this fiscal year, so a contract is expected before the end of 2026. Peru's industrial compensation rules apply to foreign defence purchases of this size, and Lockheed Martin set out a proposed Offset package in August.

    Source: Infodefensa↗ (opens in a new tab)

  • Austria5 October 2026

    Austria weighs seven fighter types to replace its early Eurofighters (opens the source in a new tab)

    The Austrian armed forces are assessing seven candidate aircraft, including Boeing's F-15EX, as a successor to the Tranche 1 Eurofighters. Austria now agrees industrial cooperation case by case on its major purchases, so a fighter buy would be its largest such negotiation yet.

    Source: Militär Aktuell↗ (opens in a new tab)

  • Colombia4 October 2026

    Colombian Army opens a US$34.5m tender for light transport aircraft (opens the source in a new tab)

    The Colombian Army has published a tender, with offers due on 26 October, to replace three retired C-212 transports. The Czech L-410 NG is reported as the front-runner. Colombia's 2025 decree on industrial Offsets applies to defence contracts from about US$3 million.

    Source: Infodefensa↗ (opens in a new tab)

  • India2 October 2026

    India's Air Force seeks proposals for 60 medium transport aircraft (opens the source in a new tab)

    The Indian Air Force has issued a request for proposals for 60 medium transport aircraft, with responses due by December. Embraer with Mahindra and Lockheed Martin with Tata are the expected bidders, and both plan Indian assembly. India's draft 2026 procurement rules would replace Offsets with a 30% Indian-content requirement on global purchases.

    Source: ThePrint↗ (opens in a new tab)
